JLPT preparation

JLPT vocabulary prep that goes beyond flashcards

WordDex combines pre-loaded JLPT decks with real-world scanning — so your exam vocabulary and the words you encounter in daily life both get full 12-round lessons in one place.

The vocabulary gap in JLPT study

JLPT preparation usually means one of two things: grinding through word lists or drilling flashcards until your hand hurts. Both work, to a point. But memorizing a definition is not the same as being able to recognize a word in a reading passage, use it in a sentence, or recall it under exam pressure.

WordDex approaches JLPT vocabulary differently. Every word — whether from a pre-loaded deck or scanned from a menu, sign, or manga page — gets a complete 12-round lesson. You practice meaning, context, sentence building, and speaking. The word then joins your Dex, where spaced repetition brings it back at optimal intervals.

This means you are not just collecting definitions. You are building active vocabulary — words you can actually use and recognize when they appear on the test or in real text.

Pre-loaded JLPT decks by level

WordDex includes curated JLPT vocabulary decks organized by level, with topics grouped to match how learners typically study. Free beginner decks and a Hiragana and Katakana reading course are there if you are not ready for exam lists yet.

Each deck word opens the same lesson format as a scanned word: 12 rounds of interactive practice covering multiple skills. There is no separate "deck mode" and "scan mode" — it is all one system, one Dex, one review schedule.

Whether you are working toward N5, N4, N3, or higher, the decks give you structured coverage. You can also describe a situation you expect on the test or in Japan, or shake your phone and let Dexy build a lesson from your learner profile.

Real-world words alongside exam vocabulary

Here is what most JLPT apps miss: the test includes vocabulary you will never find in a deck. Reading sections use words in context. Listening sections use natural speech. And the vocabulary that appears most often in real Japanese — on signs, in conversations, in media — may not match your deck order.

WordDex lets you scan any Japanese text and turn encountered words into lessons that live alongside your deck vocabulary. Your Dex becomes a combined record of structured study and real-world exposure. On exam day, you are not just recalling words from a list — you have practiced them in multiple contexts.

This dual approach is especially valuable from N3 upward, where the gap between textbook Japanese and native text becomes most noticeable. Deck study gives you breadth; scanning gives you the words that textbooks and apps tend to skip.

What a JLPT lesson looks like

When you select a word from a JLPT deck or scan one from real text, WordDex automatically builds a lesson with rounds like these:

  • Meaning recognition — match the word to its English definition
  • Reading practice — see furigana and confirm pronunciation
  • Fill-in-the-blank — complete a sentence using the word correctly
  • Sentence building — arrange words into a grammatically correct sentence
  • Speaking — say the word or sentence out loud

Twelve rounds may sound like a lot for one word, but each round takes seconds and reinforces the word from a different angle. By the end, you have done substantially more than flip a flashcard — you have practiced recognition, production, and context.

Spaced repetition in your Dex

After completing a lesson, the word enters your personal Dex. WordDex uses spaced repetition to schedule reviews — bringing words back just before you are likely to forget them. This is the same principle behind effective flashcard systems, but applied to words you have already practiced deeply through full lessons.

Your Dex shows your growing collection: deck words, scanned words, and everything in between. Over weeks and months, it becomes a visual record of your JLPT preparation — and a resource you can review anytime, not just during dedicated study sessions.
