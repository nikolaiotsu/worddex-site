Japanese

Learn Japanese from the text around you

Menus, street signs, product labels, manga panels — Japanese is everywhere once you start looking. WordDex lets you scan any word and turn it into a full lesson, with furigana support and JLPT decks when you need structure.

Why scanning beats memorizing random word lists

Japanese learners face a particular challenge: the writing system. Even after months of study, encountering a kanji compound on a restaurant menu or a train sign can feel like starting over. You might know the word when it appears in your textbook but not when it appears on a sign at 11pm when you are hungry.

WordDex closes that gap by letting you learn words in the moment you encounter them. Point your camera at the text, select the word you want, and get a complete 12-round lesson — matching, fill-in-the-blank, sentence building, and speaking — built around that specific word. When you finish, it joins your Dex for spaced repetition review.

Because you chose the word yourself, from a context you care about, it tends to stick faster than vocabulary from a generic list. The ramen shop near your apartment. The station name on your commute. The word that kept appearing in the manga you are reading.

Menus, signs, and everyday Japan

Living in or visiting Japan means constant low-stakes reading practice — if you have a tool to make use of it. A lunch menu might introduce five or six words worth learning. A pharmacy label might teach you something you will need again. A neighborhood sign might finally make a place name click.

With WordDex, each of these moments becomes a lesson instead of a frustration. You do not need to type the word, look it up in a separate dictionary, and then figure out how to remember it. Scan, learn, collect. The whole flow takes a few minutes and fits into a coffee break.

This is especially useful for learners between N5 and N3 level — past the basics but not yet comfortable reading freely. You can build vocabulary from your actual environment while gradually recognizing more of what you see around you.

Manga and native reading material

Manga is one of the most popular reasons people start learning Japanese, and also one of the most humbling. Even "easy" manga uses vocabulary and kanji that never appeared in your beginner course. WordDex is well suited to this kind of reading: scan a panel, pick the word that stumped you, and practice it until it is part of your Dex.

Over time, your collection becomes a personalized record of your reading journey. The words you struggled with in volume one will be waiting for review when you need them in volume three. You are not just collecting vocabulary — you are collecting the specific language of the stories and media you love.

Furigana support helps when you are still building kanji knowledge. WordDex can display readings alongside characters, so you can focus on meaning and usage without getting stuck on pronunciation.

JLPT decks for structured study

Real-world scanning and exam preparation work well together. WordDex includes JLPT vocabulary decks organized by level, with the first three topics free in each line. Every deck word can be turned into the same 12-round lesson format as a scanned word — so your exam vocabulary and your street vocabulary live in the same Dex.

If you are working toward N3, N2, or beyond, the decks give you structured coverage while scanning fills in the gaps with words textbooks miss. Many learners find that combining both approaches — deck study for breadth, scanning for depth — accelerates their reading confidence.

How a Japanese lesson works in WordDex

After you scan a word, WordDex automatically builds a lesson with multiple rounds of practice:

  • Meaning match — connect the word to its definition
  • Fill-in-the-blank — see the word in a sentence context
  • Sentence building — arrange words into a correct sentence
  • Speaking — practice pronunciation out loud

Each round reinforces the word from a different angle. By the end of 12 rounds, you have done more than memorize a definition — you have practiced using the word in context. The word then enters your Dex, where spaced repetition schedules future reviews at the right intervals.

Getting started with Japanese in WordDex

WordDex is free to start on iOS. You get daily practice, beginner decks, and free access to the first topics in each JLPT line. Premium unlocks unlimited lessons and full deck access.

Whether you are preparing for an exam, visiting Japan, or reading manga on your couch, the workflow is the same: see a word, scan it, learn it, collect it. Your Japanese grows from your world.
