How it works

From anything in your life to a lesson in your Dex

Four steps. A word you saw, a situation you're heading into, or a surprise from Dexy.

01

Show, tell, or shake

Scan a word, describe what's coming up, or shake your phone. Dexy wakes up either way.

02

Get a 12-round lesson

Match, fill in the blanks, build sentences, and speak out loud. A full lesson, not a definition.

03

Review until it sticks

Spaced repetition brings each word back right when you need it.

04

Collect it in your Personal Dex

Every word you catch joins a growing record of the language you've met in real life.

Three ways to start

Show it. Tell it. Shake it.

Other apps hand you their word list. WordDex builds lessons from your life: the menu you couldn't read, the interview you're nervous about, the topic you're obsessed with this week.

Show

Scan a word in the wild

Point your camera at a menu, a sign, or a manga page. WordDex reads it and builds the lesson around the word you pick.

Tell

Describe a situation

Type "ordering coffee," "my flight got cancelled," or "dinosaurs." If you care about it, it counts as study material.

Shake

Wake up Dexy

Shake your phone and Dexy, your sleepy little lesson-maker, wakes up with a surprise lesson from your interests and learner profile. He doesn't mind. It's the only way to wake him.

Why WordDex

Your words, not ours

The vocabulary that sticks is the vocabulary you actually meet. Everything you learn is something you'll see again.

Practice before it happens

Describe a date, a doctor's visit, or a hotel check-in. Get the key phrases, a model dialogue to rehearse, and teacher-style advice, tuned to a learner profile you can view and edit anytime.

Learning feels like playing

Matching games, timed Match rounds, sentence building, and streaks. Consistency is rewarded, not perfection. Miss a day? Dexy napped too.

Speak, don't just tap

Voice practice in every lesson builds the habit of using the language, not just recognizing it.

Word Scope

Go deeper on any word: real conversational usage, grammar, tone, and what a native speaker would say instead.
